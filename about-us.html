1. Profile summary

Rajadarshini Saravanan is an internationally acclaimed Bharatanatyam dancer, yoga practitioner, movement researcher, and educator, with over two decades of rigorous training and performance experience, and more than ten years of teaching across India, Europe, Asia, the UAE, the United Kingdom, the USA, and East Africa.

As the first in her bloodline to pursue Bharatanatyam, her journey has been shaped by deep dedication and divine grace & the blessings of her Guru. Trained intensively under the renowned Dr. Smt. Sheela Unnikrishnan at Sridevi Nrithyalaya, Rajadarshini has been a part of over 300 production performances, captivating audiences worldwide with her grace, precision, and profound understanding of classical Indian dance traditions.

Her interdisciplinary artistic practice explores the convergence of Dance, Yoga, and Body Kinesthetics, redefining the language of movement and stillness. Through her initiatives, Rajadarshini nurtures a global learning community, offering workshops, masterclasses, and mentorship program that explore the synergy of mind, movement, and rhythm

She is the Founder of Natyoga Darshanam, an integrative movement discipline that unites the ancient sciences of Yoga (union and awareness) and Natya (dance and expression) into a holistic pathway of embodied transformation. The framework, she pioneers injury-conscious, neuro-biomechanically informed, and emotionally intelligent training models that cultivate stillness within dynamic movement.

Drawing from her architectural background, Rajadarshini combines her research on performing spaces with climate studies, allowing her to curate thematic, nature-inspired global productions that address pressing environmental and social issues.

Her work reflects a profound sensitivity to the interaction between body, space, energy, and ecology — positioning her as a new-generation global artist who bridges tradition, science, environmental consciousness, and mindfulness.

6. Interdisciplinary research

“Performing Spaces Now & Then” – Parivartan Grant (2023–2024)

A climate-sensitive, community-driven research project exploring the evolution of traditional and modern performing spaces. The study bridges Architecture, Dance, and Ecology, with fieldwork in Thenur Village, Trichy, promoting sustainable design principles for cultural performance environments.

India Pavilion, London Design Biennale (2021–2022)

Volunteered as part of the India Pavilion at the London Design Biennale under Curator Nisha Mathew Ghosh, contributing to research and documentation of innovation in sustainability through design. This 90-day engagement deepened her understanding of spatial storytelling and the aesthetics of performative environments, marking an important milestone in her international collaboration on art, architecture, and ecology.

Certified in “Cities and Climate Change” (UN CC:Learn, UNITAR), integrating global sustainability concepts into thematic Bharatanatyam and Yoga-based productions such as “Earth in Motion”, “Ripples of Life”, and “The Plastic Pause” — innovative performances promoting ecological awareness through the language of movement across India, Europe, Asia, and the UK.
